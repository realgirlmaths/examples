Buy cheap vs buy quality

A set of worked examples showing how the breakeven calculator handles real purchasing decisions, from jewellery to footwear.

Example 1

Costume jewellery vs fine jewellery (designer brand)

A piece of costume jewellery, a bangle brass with gold plating, from a French family-owned brand costs £150. A similarly designed piece of fine jewellery, a bangle of 18k gold, from Dior costs £9000.

Input

Cheap item cost£150
Replaced every2 years
Lifespan of quality item10 years
Actual price of quality item£9,000
Return rate7%
Inflation rate2.5%
Annual appreciation6% (realistic)
Retained value at resale60%

Output

Breakeven max price at 10y £1283
Breakeven at 30 y £2206

Conclusion

Costume jewellery is the more economical choice. When market returns exceed annual appreciation of fine jewellery, holding for longer makes fine jewellery a worse investment, not better.

Example 1B

Costume jewellery vs fine jewellery (high appreciation)

Similar products to 1A, but assume the fine jewellery piece has exceptional annual appreciation.

Input

Cheap item cost£150
Replaced every2 years
Lifespan of quality item20 years
Actual price of quality item£9,000
Return rate7%
Inflation rate2.5%
Annual appreciation10% (optimistic)
Retained value at resale60%

Output

Jewellery value at 20y: £42,383
Buy cheap portfolio value: £30,838

Conclusion

If jewellery appreciation is higher than market returns, it can be optimal over a long time. These rates of return far exceed the usual real return of gold and rely on brand positioning. Rate of appreciation is the only important factor; if it is lower than market returns, even a 100% resale value does not win in the long run.

Example 2

Costume jewellery vs fine jewellery (same midmarket brand)

A bangle of 18k gold vermeil costs £279. From the same brand, with the same design, a bangle of 14k pure gold costs £2750. Annual appreciation in line with inflation only as this is not a designer brand with the ability to continually increase retail price. Resale value is low as there appears to be significant markup on the estimated weight in gold; could be lower if considering melt value.

Input

Cheap item cost£279
Replaced every2 years
Lifespan of quality item15 years
Actual price of quality item£2,750
Return rate7%
Inflation rate2.5%
Annual appreciation2.5%
Retained value at resale50%

Output

Breakeven max price £1,997

Conclusion

Fine jewellery is overpriced

Buy costume jewellery

Example 3

No resale value expected despite the high end brand; the shoes will probably eventually get worn out. Calculator does not have ability to factor in longer lifespan with repairs.

Cheap item cost£50
Replaced every1 year
Lifespan of quality item20 years
Actual price of quality item£1,785
Return rate7%
Inflation rate2.5%
Annual appreciation0%
Retained value at resale0%

Output

Breakeven max price £641
Breakeven for 10 years £388
Breakeven for 5 years £215

Conclusion

Loro Piana is overpriced

May be possible to get bespoke shoes for this price range...

Example 4

IKEA furniture vs Cotswolds Company furniture

An IKEA kitchen island (TORNVIKEN) costs £329. A similar island from the Cotswolds Company costs £999. A 15-year guarantee is included and is used as the lifespan here, but it would likely last longer. Note: CC price includes three bar stools, which are not considered here.

Input

Cheap item cost£329
Replaced every5 years
Lifespan of quality item15 years
Actual price of quality item£999
Return rate7%
Inflation rate2.5%
Annual appreciation0%
Retained value at resale0%

Output

Breakeven max price £695
Breakeven for 30 years £1059

Conclusion

Cotswolds Company furniture would have to last 30 years or more.
